Duty briefing

Welcome to the Signal Room

You're the analyst on shift. Every message below arrives as numbers, not letters — that's how computers actually store text. Your job is to move between characters and their 8-bit codes in both directions, using the extended ASCII table (codes 0–255) as your reference.

Why "extended"?
Original 7-bit ASCII only defines 128 codes (0–127) — enough for English letters, digits and punctuation. An 8th bit doubles that range to 256 codes (0–255), so codes 128–255 were added to represent accented letters, currency symbols and other characters the first 128 couldn't reach.
Why 8 bits?
One bit is a switch: on or off. Eight of them in a row give 2⁸ = 256 possible combinations — exactly enough to number every character from 0 to 255. 01001000 is one byte; it's also the letter H.
